Chapter One

Welcome to your hot tub

Thank you for choosing a hot tub from Barrel Sauna UK. This handbook explains how to set it up, how to look after the water, the shell and the timber, what to do in winter, and what can happen if these steps are skipped. Following it protects your investment and keeps your warranty valid. Please read it before your first fill.

Which tub you have

Some of the guidance in this handbook depends on the tub and the heating you chose. Find yours below so you know which lines apply to you.

Circle and Square hot tubs

A round or square tub with an insulated acrylic shell, timber or composite cladding, a fitted insulated thermo cover, a drain tap and timber steps.

Heating is a 30kW stainless steel wood burner as standard, loaded from inside or outside the tub. The stove is AISI 304 stainless steel as standard, or AISI 316 marine grade if you chose the upgrade. Your handover paperwork tells you which.

Smart Electric adds a 3kW Balboa heater with a touch screen panel and WiFi. Hybrid keeps the wood burner and adds the Balboa system. A 7kW heat pump is available on electric builds.

Double Dipper

A two person thermowood tub with a fibreglass insert, timber steps and an insulated thermo lid. It holds about 750 litres.

The Wood Fired build heats with a stove, integrated or external. The Hot and Cold build uses an electric unit that heats to 40°C or chills to 3°C.

Hydro jets and LED lighting are optional on both builds. Where this handbook talks about jets, pumps or the Balboa panel, those lines only apply if your tub has them.

How to read this handbook

  • Wood fired lines apply to every tub with a stove, including Hybrid builds.
  • Electric lines apply to Smart Electric, Hybrid and heat pump builds, and to the Double Dipper Hot and Cold build.
  • Jets and pumps lines apply only where a hydro or air system is fitted.
  • Filter lines apply only where a sand filter or UV unit is connected. The standard tub has a sand filter connection but no filter.

The two rules that matter most

  • Never drain the water while the stove is lit or still has hot embers in it. The stove must be completely cold before the water goes.
  • Never leave the tub empty or uncovered in direct sunlight. An empty shell overheats, and the damage that does is not covered by the warranty.

Chapter Two

Before your first use

Our team delivers your tub and positions it on your base. The first fill and the first heat are yours to do, and this chapter walks you through them in order.

The base

Your tub must sit on a flat, solid, level surface such as a concrete pad, paving slabs or a decking structure built to carry the load. Never place it on soil, grass or anything soft or uneven, as this can damage the structure and the shell.

  • Circle and Square: the base must carry at least 2,000kg once the tub is full and in use.
  • Double Dipper: the base must carry at least 1,150kg and measure at least 210 x 180cm.

Unpacking and checking

If any packaging remains, remove it without using a blade. A knife will score the acrylic, the cladding, the thermo cover or the metal trim. Every tub is photographed and filmed before it leaves the workshop, so if damage is found later that was caused by unpacking, we can show the tub left us without it. Check the shell, cladding, cover and fittings before you fill.

The chimney

On wood fired tubs, make sure every chimney section is fully seated and the chimney is secured so it cannot be blown over by wind. Never touch the stove or chimney while the tub is heating. The surfaces get extremely hot.

Electrics

Your tub arrives with a standard UK 13A plug fitted, or with a bare tail if you asked for it to be hardwired. Either way the connection must be made by a qualified electrician to a properly protected outdoor supply. Barrel Sauna UK does not carry out electrical work. The full specification for your electrician is in Chapter Ten.

Filling

  • Fill with clean mains water or clean well water only. Water from lakes, rivers, ponds or rainwater butts carries algae, bacteria, sand and lime that mark the shell and clog the pipework.
  • If you have connected a sand filter, connect it before you fill so only filtered water enters the tub.
  • Fill to above every jet, intake and stove connection before anything is switched on or lit.

Never fill a hot shell

If the shell has been standing in the sun or has overheated, let it cool before you fill it. Cold water on a hot shell causes a sudden temperature shock that can crack or delaminate the surface.

First checks

With the tub full and the power on, check that everything you have works: pumps, jets, LED lighting and any other fitted equipment. If your tub has a Balboa panel, download the official Balboa manual before you use it and follow it to test each function. Chapter Nine gives you the basics.

Chapter Three

Heating your tub

Wood fired

Light the stove with dry, seasoned firewood and build the fire up gradually. Never use coal, treated timber, or any fire accelerant such as lighter fluid or petrol.

  • Do not leave the tub unattended while the stove is burning.
  • Do not touch the stove, chimney or stove guard while heating or cooling. They stay hot for a long time after the fire has died down.
  • Keep children and pets away from the stove and chimney at all times.

Never drain while the stove is lit

It is strictly forbidden to drain the water while the fire is burning or while hot embers remain. The water is what protects the stove and the shell from the heat. Draining early can crack the shell, warp the stove and, in the worst case, start a fire. Let the stove go out and cool completely before the water goes.

Electric

  • 3kW Balboa heater: set the temperature you want on the panel. The water rises by about 1.5°C an hour, so plan ahead.
  • 7kW heat pump: set the temperature on the panel. The water rises by about 3°C an hour.
  • When the system is first powered up it runs a priming sequence for a few minutes before it will heat. This is normal. Chapter Nine explains it.
  • The heater will only run when the pump can move water through it. Keep the water level above the intake at all times.

Hybrid

A Hybrid tub gives you both. Heat from cold with the wood burner, then let the Balboa system hold the temperature. All of the wood fired rules above still apply whenever the stove is lit.

Double Dipper Hot and Cold

The electric unit on the Hot and Cold build heats the water to 40°C or chills it to 3°C. Set the temperature on the unit and allow time for it to reach it. Keep the unit's own manual with this handbook and follow it for operating and cleaning instructions.

Chapter Four

Water care

Clean, balanced water is the single biggest factor in how your tub looks and lasts. Poor water quality damages the shell, corrodes the stove and leaves marks that will not come off, none of which is covered by the warranty.

Which products you can use depends on your stove

The stove sits in the water, so the chemicals you add to the water reach the steel. The two grades of stainless steel react very differently.

Standard stove, AISI 304

Do not use salt, chlorine, boron or any acidic or aggressive product. These corrode 304 steel and will cause the stove to leak. Choose a spa sanitiser that contains none of them and check the label before every purchase. Non chlorine spa sanitisers, sold as active oxygen products, meet this.

Marine grade stove, AISI 316

Chlorine, bromine and salt may be used within the levels in the table below. Salt only with a salt generator fitted. Never exceed the stated levels.

Never use these in any tub

  • Swimming pool products of any kind. Pool chemistry is far too strong for a tub.
  • Acidic or aggressive solvents and descalers.
  • Any product that is not made for spas and hot tubs.

Filters and pipework

  • Where a filter is fitted, clean it at least every two weeks with a filter cleaning product, and let it dry fully before it goes back. Heavy use or more bathers will clog it sooner.
  • Jet and air systems hold water in their pipework. At every water change, fill the tub halfway, add a pipework cleaning product made for spas, and run the system for 30 minutes with the cover closed before you drain and refill.
  • Wipe the water line with a soft cloth and warm water whenever you see a mark forming. Never use scourers or abrasive cleaners on the shell.

Chapter Five

Shell, cladding and cover

The acrylic shell

Circle and Square tubs have an acrylic shell. Acrylic is tough in water and vulnerable when dry. The surface must never rise above 60°C, and the only way it gets there is an empty or uncovered tub in the sun.

  • Keep the thermo cover on whenever the tub is not in use, full or empty.
  • If you drain the tub, cover it, or refill it, the same day.
  • Do not sit or stand on the rim or the edges of the shell. The shell is shaped to carry weight in the seats, not on the lip.

What overheating does

Stress cracking across the surface, air bubbles under the finish, discolouration, and the acrylic layers separating from the backing. None of this can be repaired and none of it is covered by the warranty.

The fibreglass insert

The Double Dipper has a fibreglass insert. Treat it the same way: keep the lid on when the tub is not in use, do not leave it empty in the sun, and never use abrasive cleaners on it.

Thermowood cladding

Thermowood is heat treated for durability, but it lives outside in rain, sun and frost, so it needs the same care as the exterior of a sauna. Clean it with mild soap and warm water using a sponge or soft brush, then rinse. Let it dry completely, then apply paraffin oil in thin coats with the grain, wiping off any excess after 15 to 20 minutes. Oil once a year, or every six months in coastal or exposed locations. Skip the oiling and the timber weathers to silver grey and becomes more prone to cracking and water absorption.

Painted spruce, composite and HPL panels

Wash with mild soap and warm water, then rinse. Painted spruce can be recoated when the finish wears. Composite and HPL panels need nothing more than washing.

Never use on any surface

  • Bleach, chlorine based cleaners and strong detergents. They stain timber, corrode metal trim and damage the shell finish.
  • Pressure washers. High pressure water forces moisture into the cladding joints and can lift the finish from the shell and cover.

The thermo cover

Your insulated cover keeps the heat in and the weather out, and protects the shell from the sun. Keep it on whenever the tub is not in use. Wipe it down with mild soap and water. Do not stand on it, and do not leave it lying on the ground where it can get punctured or soaked. Gradual wear from sun and use is normal and is not a defect.

Natural timber

Timber cladding changes over time. Colour shifts, surface checks, knots that lift and slight movement with heat, moisture and frost are all natural and are not covered by the warranty. Regular oiling slows every one of them.

Chapter Six

Winter and frost

Frozen water expands. In a tub that means a cracked shell, a split stove, burst pipework and a wrecked pump. None of it is covered, and all of it is avoidable.

Wood fired tubs

  • In freezing weather, drain the tub fully after each use. Once the stove is completely cold, open the drain tap and the base plug and let every drop go.
  • Empty all pipework, jets, the pump and the stove connections. Water left anywhere in the system will freeze.
  • If the tub cannot be fully drained, use an antifreeze made for spa systems. Never use car antifreeze.
  • Put the cover back on the empty tub.

Electric, Hybrid and heat pump tubs

  • Leave the tub full and set the controller to Winter Mode, with a minimum water temperature of 10°C. The system will keep the water above freezing.
  • If you drain an electric tub for the winter, empty every system as above and use spa antifreeze where water cannot be removed.

After a winter stood empty

If a wood fired tub has been drained and left outside over winter, do not start the massage pump or the circulation pump until the water in the tub has reached at least 20°C. Water left in the pump housing may have frozen. Starting the pump against ice damages the internals and can stop the pump working for good.

Still the same rule

Never drain the tub while the stove is hot or contains embers. Draining for frost is no exception. Let it go out and cool first.

Other seasons

  • Autumn: clear leaves and debris from around the base and off the cover. They hold moisture against the cladding.
  • Coastal areas: salt in the air speeds up wear. Rinse the cladding with fresh water now and then and oil thermowood at least twice a year.
  • Long periods unused: drain, clean, oil the cladding and cover the tub before you leave it.

Chapter Seven

Safety and precautions

People

  • Children may only use the tub under adult supervision, and never alone.
  • Anyone pregnant, or with a heart, circulation or skin condition, should speak to a doctor before using a hot tub.
  • Never dive, and never put your head under the water. Pump intakes and jets can pull in hair.
  • Do not sit on the rim or the edges of the shell.
  • Keep animals out of the tub and away from it.

Electrics

  • Never touch the plug, the panel or any electrical equipment with wet hands.
  • All electrical parts must stay protected from water. Report any damage to cables, plugs or enclosures before the tub is used again.
  • Test the RCD once a month by pressing its test button. It should trip every time.

Fire

  • Never leave a burning stove unattended.
  • Never use flammable liquids to light or boost the fire.
  • Do not touch the stove or chimney while hot.
  • Keep a fire extinguisher within reach of a wood fired tub.

Stop use immediately if

  • The RCD trips and will not reset, or there is any smell of burning from the electrics.
  • You see a crack, bubble or split in the shell.
  • The stove or chimney is loose, leaning or leaking smoke into the tub area.
  • Water is leaking from the stove or the pipework.

Contact us before the tub is used again. Details are on the last page.

Chapter Eight

Routine care and checklist

Suggested timetable

  • After every use: put the cover back on. If the tub is wood fired and frost is forecast, drain it once the stove is cold.
  • Weekly: test pH and sanitiser and adjust to the targets in Chapter Four. Wipe the water line.
  • Every two weeks: clean and dry the filter, where one is fitted.
  • Monthly: test the RCD. Check the cover for damage and the cladding for lifting or loose fixings.
  • Every water change: flush the jet and air pipework for 30 minutes before draining.
  • Yearly: oil thermowood cladding (twice a year on the coast). Check every chimney section is seated and secure. Have the electrics inspected.

Quick reference

  • Cover on whenever the tub is not in use.
  • Stove completely cold before the water goes.
  • No salt, chlorine or boron with a 304 stove. No pool products in any tub.
  • pH 7.2 to 7.6.
  • Winter Mode on electric tubs, full drain on wood fired tubs in frost.
  • No pumps after a winter stood empty until the water is above 20°C.
  • Oil thermowood once a year.
  • Never fill a hot shell. Never leave an empty shell in the sun.

Chapter Nine

Your Balboa panel

This chapter applies to Smart Electric, Hybrid and heat pump tubs. It covers the basics you will use every week. The official Balboa manual covers everything else, and you should download it before first use.

Powering on and priming

  • When power is applied the panel runs a set up sequence. This is normal.
  • Priming mode lasts four to five minutes and lets the pumps fill with water before heating starts. During priming you can run the pumps with the Jets or Aux buttons, or Light for the circulation pump.
  • If water is still not flowing after two minutes, switch off, wait, and try again. Do not try more than five times.
  • Never run a pump dry for more than two minutes. It will be damaged.
  • When priming ends the panel briefly shows the set temperature. The actual water temperature appears once the system has measured flow.

Setting the temperature

Press the temperature up or down button while the number is flashing. Press Light, or wait, to leave the setting screen.

Pumps and jets

  • Tap Jets 1 once for low speed and again for high speed. A second pump uses Jets 2 or Aux.
  • Pumps switch themselves off after 15 to 30 minutes unless you tap again.

Modes

  • Ready: holds your set temperature automatically.
  • Rest: energy saving, heats less often.
  • Hold or Standby: pauses everything, for cleaning the filter.
  • Drain mode: prepares the system for draining.
  • Winter Mode: keeps the water above the minimum temperature through freezing weather. Set it before the first frost.

Utilities

The utility menus give you the RCD trip test, the fault log and the configuration options. If the panel shows a code you do not recognise, note it down and check the Balboa manual, then contact us if it does not clear.

LED lighting

On electric and hybrid tubs the lighting is controlled from the panel, colour, brightness and modes. On wood fired tubs the lighting has its own push button on the side of the tub. Press to cycle through the colours.

Chapter Ten

For your electrician

Hand this chapter to the electrician connecting your tub. Barrel Sauna UK supplies the tub with its electrical accessories fitted and wired to a single supply cable. The connection to your property is the electrician's job.

Compliance

  • All work must comply with BS 7671 (IET Wiring Regulations) and Part P of the Building Regulations, and be carried out by a competent registered electrician (NICEIC, NAPIT, ECA or equivalent).
  • Dedicated supply circuit. No extension leads, plug adaptors or multi socket boards, ever.
  • 30mA RCD protection, Type A. Where inverters or variable speed drives are fitted without their own DC leakage detection, follow the equipment maker's advice, which may call for Type F or Type B.
  • MCB curve C, to tolerate pump motor inrush.
  • All outdoor wiring, enclosures and connections IP rated for outdoor and damp conditions. Fixed outdoor runs in NYY-J or equivalent, flexible connections in damp areas in H07RN-F. Copper conductors, insulation rated 70°C or above.
  • A weatherproof rotary isolator, IP54 or better, within sight of the tub, 1.5 to 3m away and outside zones 0 and 1.
  • Correct earthing, with supplementary bonding to all accessible metalwork around the tub. Verify earth continuity before first use.
  • Distribution boards, sockets, control and junction boxes outside the splash zones.

Read the label first

The specification label inside the service panel shows a generic power range for the model and a set of tick boxes for the equipment actually fitted. Calculate the connected load from the ticked items only, never from the generic range. The label on a wood fired tub with a hydro pump and lighting will typically show a 1100W pump and 100W of LEDs ticked, a connected load of about 1200W, which is suitable for a properly protected 13A supply.

Wood fired tubs with pump and lights only

On these tubs the electricity powers the accessories only. The water is heated by the stove.

Option A, 13A plug

  • Where the fitted load is within 13A.
  • Correctly fitted UK plug with a 13A fuse into an outdoor rated, RCD protected socket.
  • Proper cable grip and strain relief.
  • Polarity: brown live, blue neutral, green and yellow earth.

Option B, hardwired

  • Wired into a suitable outdoor rated fused spur or isolator.
  • RCD or RCBO protection, correct earthing, cable protection.
  • Outdoor rated enclosure and a local isolation point.
  • No exposed joints, no temporary connections.

Before switching on

  • Fill the tub. Make sure the pump intake is submerged and no pump can run dry.

Chapter Eleven

Your warranty

The terms and periods of your warranty are set out in the Warranty Agreement you received at handover. This chapter tells you what keeps it valid and what puts it at risk.

What keeps it valid

  • Inspect the tub at delivery and report any issue within 48 hours.
  • Follow the care, water, winter and safety guidance in this handbook.
  • Have the electrical connection made by a qualified electrician and keep the certificate.
  • Report a suspected defect within 14 days of noticing it.

What is not covered

  • Damage from improper use, poor maintenance or ignoring this handbook.
  • An empty or uncovered tub left in the sun, and the cracking, bubbling, discolouration or delamination that follows.
  • Use of unsuitable chemicals, including pool products, acidic or aggressive solvents, or any product we have said not to use.
  • Water left out of balance, and the scale, water line marks and corrosion that follows.
  • Salt, chlorine, boron or aggressive chemicals used with a 304 stove.
  • Permitted chemical levels exceeded with a 316 stove.
  • Frost damage to the shell, stove, pipework or pump where the tub was not drained, put into Winter Mode or protected with spa antifreeze.
  • Mechanical damage: scratches, dents, impacts, or overloading such as sitting on the rim.
  • Non original or incompatible equipment fitted to the tub.
  • Incorrect electrical connection, or work not to the standards in Chapter Ten.
  • Natural timber ageing: colour change, cracks, knot loss and movement from sun, moisture and frost.
  • Normal wear of the thermo cover from use, sun or poor care.
  • Wear of filters, seals, gaskets, glued parts and other consumables.
  • Damage from fire, electrical faults, flood, storm, accident or other events beyond our control.

Barrel Sauna UK reserves the right to decline a claim where the tub or its equipment has been used against the guidance in this handbook.

How to make a claim

  • Email info@barrelsaunauk.com within 14 days of identifying the issue.
  • Include your order number, photos or video of the affected area, and a clear written description of the problem.
  • Include evidence of correct installation, water care or electrical work where it is relevant to the claim.

We assess every claim in good faith. Where a defect is confirmed we may provide guidance, supply replacement parts, recommend a local professional, or agree another resolution with you.
